How we plan to climb the Verifiable Quantum Circuit Compilation leaderboard

The competition asks for smaller quantum circuits for three real problems. Every circuit is checked automatically by a classical simulator, so we can iterate fast and trust a passing result. This page explains the problem, the scoring, where the field stands, and our plan.

The score is how many times cheaper our circuits are than Google's reference circuits, averaged across the three tasks with a geometric mean. Because it is a geometric mean, a 1% saving on any task is worth the same, about 0.33% of the final score. Top 1% needs about 14% total saving across the tasks (multiplied together). #1 needs about 59%.

Score = ( ref₁/cost₁ × ref₂/cost₂ × ref₃/cost₃ )^(1/3)

Why these circuits can be checked

General quantum circuits cannot be simulated classically beyond about 40 qubits. These benchmarks use a restricted family called kickmix circuits: reversible classical logic (NOT, CNOT, Toffoli), phase gates, and measurement-based clean-up. Each instruction maps one bit string to one bit string plus a phase, so a normal CPU can run the circuit on millions of test inputs. If it is right on every basis input, linearity makes it right on every superposition too.

Toffoli gates are the expensive resource on an error-corrected quantum computer. Qubits set the size of the machine. Every task's cost is built from these two numbers. The trick drawn above (compute an AND with one Toffoli, erase it by measuring) appears in every winning circuit.

The three tasks

1 · Fusion energy: phase by 1/√x

public best 21.75×

A simulation of fusion fuel needs the Coulomb energy 1/distance for every pair of electrons. The circuit gets a 22-bit number x (1 to 3,133,452) and must apply the phase e^(2πi/√x) to about 7 significant digits, leaving no scratch behind. It resembles the classic "fast inverse square root" (a first guess plus Newton refinement), made reversible.

cost = qubits × (Toffolis + T-gates/4)
Today's best public circuit
  • 62 qubits, 1,540 Toffoli-equivalents (reference: 260 and 7,988)
  • Degree-2 polynomial over 256 lookup segments
  • Accuracy budget fully used (0.9993 of the tolerance)
Our levers
  • Each qubit removed: about −1.6% cost. Target 62 → 60.
  • Merge the two phase passes: 41 → about 35 rotations, −2 to −4%
  • Every input is tested and zero failures are allowed, so there is no gambling

realistic headroom −5 to −10%

2 · Optimization: Reed–Solomon decoder for DQI

public best 65.2×

Decoded Quantum Interferometry turns a hard optimisation problem (Optimal Polynomial Intersection) into error-correction decoding. The circuit receives 70 syndromes of a Reed–Solomon code over GF(2¹²) and must recover a ±1 sign from up to 35 errors, reversibly and cleanly. Occasional failures are allowed but are charged through a repetition factor R.

cost = R(failure rate) × qubits² × Toffolis
Today's best public circuit
  • 892 qubits, 395k Toffolis per shot, 0 failures (reference: 1,893 and 5.73M)
  • Overwrites the syndromes in place; 42-AND field multiplier
  • Design unchanged since 26 Oct; only −2.7% from patches last week
Our levers
  • Qubits count squared: each qubit ≈ −0.22% cost
  • Profile the stages first, which nobody has published
  • Cheaper GF(2¹²) multiplier: each AND saved ≈ −2%
  • Redesign the key-equation core (high risk, high reward)

realistic headroom −10 to −15% (ceiling −35%)

3 · Chemistry: block-encoding FeMoCo

public best 11.2×

FeMoCo is the enzyme cluster that fixes nitrogen. Simulating it means loading a huge compressed table of numbers (a tensor factorisation) into the quantum computer with lookup circuits (QROM) and Givens rotations. We choose the factorisation and design the angle-loading circuits. The rest is priced by formula.

cost = λ_eff × Toffolis_total × qubits_total
Today's best public
  • λ_eff 46.89: a proven floor is 46.79, so there is nothing left there
  • 427 qubits, locked by the cost formula
  • 14,966 Toffolis, of which 8,304 are in the angle loaders we design
Our levers
  • New loophole found in debate: a table with ≤ about 650 rows saves one address bit and roughly halves loader cost. Model: 11.2× → about 14.3×
  • Smarter rounding so the lookup tables compress
  • Unproven: the smaller table must still be accurate to 20 mHa

realistic headroom −15 to −25%

Our solution, step by step

0 · Get on the board

this week

Rebuild the best public circuits, re-check them with the unmodified official verifiers under our own random salt, and submit (≈25.1, roughly top 100–500). The same notebook is our test harness: every later candidate is scored with it before submission.

1 · Cheap, likely wins → top 1%

weeks 1–3

Run three experiments in parallel, each with a go/no-go date: fit the smaller chemistry table (stop if error before rounding exceeds 16 mHa), the invsqrt rotation merge and qubit squeeze, and a stage-by-stage profile of the DQI decoder.

2 · One shared synthesis engine

weeks 2–7

All three experts agreed on this: one tool that minimises the AND gates in a fixed Boolean or GF(2) map (exact SAT search on small windows, flip-graph search on multipliers). It applies to the chemistry angle loaders, the DQI field multiplier and the invsqrt table decoder.

3 · DQI redesign

weeks 3–9, if the profile says go

Fewer multiplications per step, an in-place faster key-equation core, and a qubit squeeze toward 860–875. Only accept a rare-case shortcut of probability p if it saves more than 1.86×10⁸·p Toffolis.

4 · Lock in and claim prizes

weeks 9–10

Choose a best and a safer final submission, track per-task records for the three $10k single-task prizes, keep every circuit reproducible from open code, and decide on team mergers before 13 Jan.

Ground rules

Nothing is submitted unless it passes the unmodified official verifier. We do not use tricks that pass the checker but are physically invalid (for example, measuring the chemistry selection register). We do not try to reach the hidden test salt or any non-public data. Any verifier bug we find is reported, not exploited. These would get a team disqualified and forfeit prizes.
